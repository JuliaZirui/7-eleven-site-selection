An AI-Assisted Research Workflow for Retail Expansion

Turns fragmented public data into traceable site-screening recommendations and targeted follow-up research.
1. Situation
Retail expansion teams
shortlisting sites in a new city
Strategy consultants
desk phase of market-entry work
Location analysts
commercial and public-sector site studies
Why desk research falls short today
  • Labour-intensive collection: fragmented sources gathered and cross-checked by hand, site by site.
  • Misdirected follow-up: verification effort goes to gaps that would not change the decision.
  • Inconsistent assessments: different sources and criteria; results hard to compare or reproduce.
  • False precision: a single ranking hides data gaps and untested assumptions.
  • Missing data read as zero: no data for a site makes it look free of competition.
2. How it works System architecture
Highlights
01
Deterministic scoring, bounded AI
Every score is computed deterministically. AI acts only within code-enforced budgets and guardrails; only reviewer-approved corrections reach the data.
02
City-wide benchmarking
Scales (P10 / P90) are calibrated on 139 Berlin S/U-Bahn stations, so each site is measured against the whole city, not just the other two.
03
Evaluation-led AI adoption
Both AI components passed pre-registered evaluations first. Rules matched the LLM on all 14 ordering decisions, so rules decide follow-ups and the LLM drafts field questions; the data audit met thresholds fixed in advance.
04
Production-grade engineering
241 automated tests, fully reproducible offline runs, and failure handling with rule fallback.

Grey dots: inner-Berlin S/U-Bahn stations; bands and dashed lines: each day type's P10 / P90. Brandenburger Tor is in the city's least contested tenth on both day types (9.7 / 13.9 competitors per 10,000 people); Schönhauser Allee in the most crowded tenth (85.9 / 91.4).
