## 8. Method caveats that must travel with any figure quoted from this report

- **Radius.** 400m is the convenience-retail industry's general primary-trade-area convention, *not* a 7-Eleven-specific standard — no 7-Eleven catchment radius is publicly disclosed.
- **Preisklasse.** All three nodes are DB Preisklasse 4. This measures a station's importance to DB rail operations, not footfall — Brandenburger Tor's landmark tourist volume is not captured by it.
- **Visitors** are estimated, not counted: annual de+en Wikipedia pageviews of every notable sight and venue for which the node's station is a main S/U gateway (straight-line, so river crossings are ignored), shared among co-gateway stations, times ~10 visitors per pageview (geometric mean over 37 Berlin attractions with published counts; single-site error typically ×2.6). Tourist landmarks run nearer 4 visitors per pageview and zoos / family museums nearer 40–50, so landmark-heavy nodes may be over-stated and family-attraction nodes under-stated; free open-air sites with no count of their own (Brandenburger Tor) are outside the fit. Where an annual figure is known it replaces the conversion: the 37 published counts (e.g. Reichstag 1.85 million, not the 4.8 million its fame implies) and capacity-based venues in `db/raw/calibration/venue_attendance_verified.csv` — Berghain ~115,000 a year (56,000–240,000: 1,500 capacity, ~34 opening hours per weekend, ~11-hour stays), where its fame implied 5.9 million. Other clubs without a known figure are still converted from fame and may be over-stated.
- **Workers** count only registrations whose nearest S/U station is this node's (straight line; walking routes checked for every large employer) — e.g. ~30% of the people working in Brandenburger Tor's circle are nearer Unter den Linden or Friedrichstraße. Residents are not station-assigned: their top-up shopping is a walk from home, not a station trip. Headcounts are IHK registrations converted at each size class's geometric midpoint, except every registration of 500+ employees in a candidate circle, which is replaced by its verified on-site headcount (DZ BANK's Berlin office, not DZ BANK AG; Zalando's stated campus headcount; TikTok's reported Berlin staff; a delivery company's coworking desk rather than its couriers). The reference stations' workers are *not* verified and stay as registered, so their distribution — and the demand scale's P90 — likely sits high; candidates are scored conservatively against it. Registrations with unknown size are left out, not imputed.
- **Catchment residents** are area-weighted from MSS 2025 Planungsraum populations (as of 31.12.2024), assuming uniform density within each Planungsraum. MSS is a monitoring-report population base, one step removed from the EWR register export (currently unreachable at LOR level).
- **Peak competitor sets** are defined by format: morning = bakery, café, supermarket, fuel-station shop; evening = späti/kiosk, fast food, supermarket, fuel-station shop. Chemists, beverage stores and delis are in neither. A competitor is dropped from a peak only if its OSM opening hours show it closed for the whole window on a representative Wednesday; unknown hours (all Overture-only competitors) count as open. `PH` rules are ignored (no holiday calendar).
- **Weekday vs. weekend.** Residents count on both day types. Workers at the weekend = weekday on-site headcount × the sector's share working weekends at least occasionally (Destatis Mikrozensus 2023: hospitality 70%, arts/entertainment 55%, trade 47%, health/social 46%, all employees 27%; office sectors 10% — an assumption, no Destatis figure) × 0.5 (assumed share of weekend days such a worker is on site). Visitors: each venue's annual visitors spread over its open days — OSM hours where tagged, otherwise every day, except clubs, which count on Friday/Saturday nights only (Berghain opens Friday midnight to Monday morning). Day attractions get no weekend uplift (no source); a weekend tourist surge would favour visitor-heavy nodes further.
- **Capped scales.** A node beyond the reference stations' P90 scores 100 however far beyond it is: on a weekend day both Brandenburger Tor (~19,900 people) and Warschauer Straße (~28,900) exceed the P90 of 13,900, so the weekend demand criterion does not separate them — see the people counts in §3 for the real gap.
- **Sunday trading.** Competitors without OSM hours follow the Berliner Ladenöffnungsgesetz on Sundays: supermarkets, spätis, beverage and chemist shops closed unless inside a station (station shops may trade without time limit — as could a station-embedded 7-Eleven); bakeries 7–16; cafés, fast food and fuel shops open. Unknown-hours fast food counts as open at night, so the weekend night count is an upper bound.
- **Freshness** is judged on each source's data-as-of date against a per-source age limit (90 days for live/monthly sources, 365 for attraction pageviews, ~3 years for the biennial MSS population base), not on fetch date.
- **OSM competitor coverage** is volunteer-mapped; a low count is a candidate for under-mapping as much as for low competition.
